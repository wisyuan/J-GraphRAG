\section{Replacement Experiments (RQ4)}
\label{sec:replacement}

\subsection{Protocol}
\label{sec:replacement:protocol}

\textbf{Replacement--retention}: we faithfully reimplement the non-LLM parts
of each framework (graph construction logic + retrieval algorithm) and swap
only the LLM extraction for the hybrid pipeline of \S\ref{sec:method:offline}.
The originals cannot be run locally (Fast-GraphRAG is broken), so we convert
via the archived leaderboard numbers plus a conversion factor:
\begin{align}
\text{retention} &= \mathrm{ACC}(\text{replaced}, \text{our judge}) \times \text{factor} \;/\; \text{leaderboard original},\\
\text{factor} &= \text{leaderboard RAG mean} \;/\; \text{our co-run B0}
\quad (\text{medical } 1.005,\ \text{novel } 0.885).
\end{align}
The verdict line is $\geq 0.9$. Evaluation: medical 56 questions (14 per level
$\times$ 4 levels) + novel 48 questions, DeepSeek judge (noise
$\pm 0.03$--$0.05$).

\subsection{L4 evaluation-protocol correction (Phase 54)}
\label{sec:replacement:l4fix}

The strict judge asks whether the generated answer conveys the same information
as the gold answer. This is appropriate for L1--L3 but structurally biased
against L4 (Creative Generation), whose gold answer is only \emph{one} valid
realization---on novel L4 even the dense baseline B0 scored $0/12$, while the
official leaderboard reports 23.8--48.3\% for the same level (the benchmark
evaluates Creative Generation with an Accuracy / Factual Score / Coverage
rubric, not strict gold matching). We therefore re-judged all stored L4 answers
with a matching rubric judge (binary rule: accuracy $\geq 0.5$ and zero factual
contradictions), leaving L1--L3 verdicts untouched:

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{Retention before $\to$ after the L4 protocol correction.}
\label{tab:l4fix}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcc@{}}
\toprule
Arm & medical & novel \\
\midrule
LightRAG-J final & 1.011 $\to$ \textbf{1.207} & 1.104 $\to$ \textbf{1.227} \\
HippoRAG-J $w{=}0.3$ & 0.996 $\to$ \textbf{1.245} & 0.751 $\to$ 0.816 \\
HippoRAG-J $w{=}0.5$ & --- $\to$ 1.107 & 0.881 $\to$ \textbf{0.914} \\
HippoRAG-J $w{=}0.8$ & --- $\to$ 1.218 & 0.881 $\to$ \textbf{0.947} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Three checks bound the correction's validity. (i) \emph{Calibration}: under the
rubric judge our B0 anchor lands near the official plain-RAG L4 numbers (novel
0.333 vs.\ 0.385--0.415; medical 0.714 vs.\ 0.589--0.606), a deviation of the
same order as judge noise. (ii) \emph{Threshold robustness}: the binary rule's
threshold can vary in $[0.3, 0.6]$ without changing any conclusion.
(iii) \emph{Residual diagnosis}: retrieval is not the remaining
bottleneck---key entities appear in the top-10 chunks for 71--100\% of L4
questions; the residual L4 gap is generation-side faithfulness (the rubric
judge catches fabricated details in the generated text). Caveats: the recompute
mixes our rubric L4 with leaderboard averages produced by the official metric
(bounded to $\pm 0.12$ by the anchor check), and the re-judge is a single run
(one novel L4 question $\approx \pm 0.021$ total ACC), so HippoRAG-J novel
sitting just above the 0.9 line should be read as \emph{retained, within
noise}.

A follow-up on the full 67-question novel Creative Generation set (Phase 55,
same rubric judge plus a task-adaptive generation prompt) sharpens the
picture in both directions: the dense baseline reaches 0.448---slightly above
the official plain-RAG L4 range, confirming the corrected measurement
chain---yet the graph arm trails it at L4 (0.373; discordant pairs 5:10).
Graph expansion thus helps L1--L3 but not creative generation, where
scene-specific narrative chunks are diluted by hub chunks; we scope the
multi-hop claim to discrimination tasks accordingly.

\subsection{Comparative analysis}

Under the corrected L4 protocol both algorithms clear the 0.9 line in both
domains (dual-level 1.207/1.227; best PPR arms 1.245/0.947). The stability
argument for dual-level nevertheless stands: PPR's novel retention swings
0.816--0.947 across passage weights and needs per-graph recalibration, while
dual-level (MAX aggregation + naive interleaving) reaches its number without
any per-graph tuning. The production default is dual-level, with PPR as a
tunable option.
