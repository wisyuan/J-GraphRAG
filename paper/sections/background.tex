\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}

\subsection{J-Lens and the J-space}

The Jacobian Lens computes, for each layer's residual stream, an average
first-order causal effect $J_l$ (averaging Jacobians over a thousand-scale
corpus), mapping intermediate activations into the unembedding space to read
out top-$k$ tokens. The original work
(transformer-circuits.pub/2026/workspace) shows these readouts satisfy the
functional properties of a global workspace: reportable, modulable, carrying
intermediate reasoning, generalizable, and selective. Three empirical findings
matter most for this work:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Intermediate entities of internal reasoning enter the workspace}
(in a multi-hop question the model first produces \emph{spider} and then
answers the number of legs)---entity-level representations exist.
\item \textbf{The paired-question protocol}: the question form determines what
enters the workspace---content used only implicitly does not enter; content
must be explicitly named. This dictates our prompt design principles
(\S\ref{sec:method:offline}) and uniformly explains all cloze-style failures
(\S\ref{sec:math:cloze}).
\item \textbf{The J-Lens is single-token}---multi-word concepts/entities
require corpus-statistical merging (\S\ref{sec:method:offline}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Division of labor with interpretability research.}
They prove the workspace exists and is causally privileged (requiring
interventional experiments); we prove the structure can be harvested at low
engineering cost---using \emph{corpus-grounded validation} in place of
\emph{interventional validation} (\S\ref{sec:discussion:methodology}).

\subsection{Benchmark and evaluation}

GraphRAG-Bench (arXiv:2506.05690) provides two domains: \emph{medical} (NCCN
guidelines, 957 chunks) and \emph{novel} (19th-century fiction, 4391 chunks),
with four task levels: L1 fact retrieval, L2 complex reasoning, L3 contextual
summarization, L4 creative generation. The original leaderboard numbers
(GPT-4o-mini extraction and evaluation) are archived in
\texttt{data/m6/graphrag\_bench\_leaderboard.json}. Our evaluation uses bge-m3
retrieval with a DeepSeek judge, anchored to the leaderboard by a conversion
factor from a co-run B0 baseline (\S\ref{sec:replacement:protocol}).
